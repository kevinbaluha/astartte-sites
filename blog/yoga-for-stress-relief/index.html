Yoga for Stress Relief, Body Awareness & Everyday Wellness

A consistent yoga practice can create an opportunity to slow down, reconnect with your body, notice how you're feeling and become more aware of what supports your overall well-being.

Stress, busy schedules, emotional challenges and everyday responsibilities can sometimes leave us disconnected from the signals our bodies are giving us. We may not notice how much tension we're carrying until the body asks us to pay attention.

You may notice that certain activities, foods, environments or stressful experiences leave you feeling uncomfortable, tired, tense or depleted. Rather than immediately judging the experience, yoga and mindfulness encourage curiosity.

What would help me feel more comfortable and grounded?

Have I been ignoring something my body has been communicating?

This kind of awareness can gradually help you develop a more compassionate relationship with your body.

Stress doesn't only affect our thoughts. We may experience it physically through muscle tension, restlessness, fatigue, changes in breathing, digestive discomfort or difficulty settling down.

Gentle yoga gives the body an opportunity to move without requiring high-intensity exercise.

Certain gentle yoga positions can also feel comforting when the abdomen feels tense or uncomfortable.

Practices such as Child's Pose, knees-to-chest variations, gentle twists and relaxed breathing may help some people feel more comfortable and connected with the abdominal area.

The goal isn't to force the body into a particular result. Instead, yoga gives you an opportunity to slow down and notice what feels supportive.

If abdominal discomfort, bloating, pain or digestive symptoms are persistent or significant, they should be discussed with an appropriate healthcare professional rather than relying on yoga to determine their cause.

One of the things I want people to understand about yoga is that you don't have to perform a pose exactly as you see it in a book, photograph or video.

Props, blankets, pillows, bolsters, chairs and modifications can make practices more accessible and comfortable.

It is to discover how movement, breath and awareness can support your body.

This is especially important in private yoga sessions, where the practice can be adapted according to your experience, comfort and goals.

Yoga and Meditation naturally complement one another.

Movement can help prepare the body to become still, while meditation creates an opportunity to notice thoughts, emotions and sensations with greater awareness.
