The Power of Your Own Energy

You may recognize it through your breathing, posture, emotions, thoughts and level of vitality. You may feel open and expressive in one moment, quiet and inward in another. Sometimes your energy feels grounded and flowing; at other times, it may feel depleted, scattered, guarded or overwhelmed.

These changes are part of being human. Learning to notice them without judgment is an important part of energy awareness.

Have you noticed that people sometimes respond to you differently depending on how you are feeling?

When you feel engaged, inspired and connected with yourself, you may speak more freely, make more eye contact and appear more available for connection. When you are grieving, anxious, exhausted or emotionally overwhelmed, you may naturally become quieter or more protective of your space.

People may respond to these visible and subtle signals, even when nothing has been spoken. Animals may notice them too. They often respond to changes in our movement, voice, attention and emotional presence.

This does not mean you must always appear positive so that others will be drawn to you. Your sadness, vulnerability and need for solitude deserve to be honored. Energy awareness is not about performing happiness. It is about recognizing what you are experiencing and choosing how you want to care for yourself within it.

In Landmark terminology, the “occurring world” refers to the way life appears to us through our interpretations, experiences and internal state.

Two people can encounter the same situation and experience it very differently. Even our own view of a situation can change when we are rested, supported and grounded instead of frightened, depleted or overwhelmed.

When your internal state changes, the world around you may seem to change as well. You may recognize possibilities that you could not see before, communicate differently or become more willing to receive support.

The people around you do not necessarily need to change for you to begin having a different experience. Sometimes changing how you relate to your thoughts, emotions and energy opens an entirely new way of participating in your life.

It is important to distinguish between energy that can be gently redirected and emotions that need to be felt, understood or supported.

A temporary sense of restlessness may shift through breathing, walking, music or movement. Deep grief, trauma, anger or depression may require more time and compassionate support. These experiences should not be treated as failures of intention or signs that you are vibrating incorrectly.

You cannot always will difficult feelings away—and you should not have to.

Sometimes the most powerful use of your energy is allowing yourself to acknowledge what is true. You can listen to the sadness, rest with the exhaustion or seek support for what feels too heavy to carry alone.

Personal power includes knowing when to generate movement and when to remain still.

Many spiritual, yogic and energy-healing traditions use the elements as symbols for different qualities within us. You can work with these qualities through meditation and visualization.

Imagine yourself connected to the earth beneath you. Feel the support of the chair, floor or ground. Allow your breathing to settle and notice the physical weight of your body.

Earth energy can remind you of stability, presence and belonging.
